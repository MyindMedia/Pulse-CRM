## Why

Studio owners keep their real books outside Pulse, usually in a spreadsheet a bookkeeper fills in each month: a journal of paired debit and credit lines and a tab of financial statements typed in by hand. The statements are mostly hard-coded numbers, so nothing proves they agree with the journal or the bank. The first tenant we looked at (a recording studio, July 2026) shows the problem in miniature: the income statement leaves out a 19.00 software charge that is in the journal, the retained earnings line is a plug formula that forces the balance sheet to balance, the journal's cash is 630.00 away from the bank statement, and the beginning cash label names the wrong month.

The finance change `finance-ledger-reconciliation-reports` explicitly left a full double-entry general ledger out of scope. This change adds it, so Pulse can give every studio owner a concise, accurate, branded report built from a ledger that balances, and can say plainly where the owner's own statements disagree with it.

## What Changes

- A double-entry ledger per studio: a chart of accounts, journal entries (one document per transaction, lines in integer cents, debits equal credits enforced by one shared validator), opening balances, the statements the owner's books reported, and bank statement balances for reconciliation.
- A pure statements engine (no Convex imports, unit tested): trial balance, balance sheet as of a date with retained earnings shown as a derived line and an A = L + E check that never uses a plug, income statement by account, direct-method cash flow classified by counter-account, and a list of checks (balanced entries, A = L + E, cash per ledger against the bank, cash flow tie-out, unclassified accounts, missing receipts, uncleared clearing accounts, negative cash, entries outside the period, duplicates).
- A workbook importer: a pure parser for the journal and statements tabs that normalizes account, category, payment type and date variants and reports every normalization and anomaly as a warning with its source row, a default studio chart of accounts seeded from the workbook (the studio's own account names kept), idempotency by a content hash per entry plus an import batch id, and a CLI with `--dry-run` (counts, warnings, reported vs recomputed table) and `--apply`.
- A Convex API (`convex/ledger.ts`) for the branded report UI (phase B) and the accounting agent (phase C): periods, statements (reported, recomputed, variances, checks), the journal (paginated and filtered), accounts, add and void entries, link receipts, bank reconciliation, and an explicit, idempotent bridge from an existing `expenses` row to a balanced entry.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities
- `finance/ledger`: chart of accounts, journal entries, opening balances, statements, checks and reconciliation.
- `finance/books-import`: importing an owner's monthly books workbook, with warnings and a reported vs recomputed reconciliation.

### Modified Capabilities
- None. Existing expense, banking and P&L paths are unchanged; `ledger.postFromExpense` is opt in.

## Impact

- Schema: five new org-scoped tables (`ledgerAccounts`, `journalEntries`, `openingBalances`, `reportedStatements`, `bankStatementBalances`). None is mirrored to devices. All are listed in `ORG_TABLES` (they die with the workspace) and kept by `orgReset` (they are real books, not staged demo history).
- Access: reads follow `insights.read` (owner, manager unless the owner turned money off, accountant, agency owner and admin acting as the studio). Writes require `insights.read` and an owner or manager seat (agency owner and admin act as the studio under the existing scope rules).
- No real tenant data is committed. Test fixtures are generated by a builder with neutral names and no account numbers.

## Non-goals (phase A)

- The branded report UI (phase B) and the accounting agent (phase C).
- Accrual adjustments, depreciation, closing entries and multi-currency.
- Auto-posting existing expenses for existing studios. The bridge is explicit.
- Deciding how the 630.00 cash variance is resolved. Pulse surfaces it; the owner or their bookkeeper decides.
